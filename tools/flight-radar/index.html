How it works

Aircraft equipped with ADS-B Out (Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast) broadcast their GPS position, altitude, ground speed, and callsign once or twice a second. Receivers on the ground, many run by volunteers, pick up those broadcasts and share them with community networks. Every 10 seconds, this page asks FlyItalyADSB, a network that also merges in data from adsb.lol and theairtraffic.com, for the aircraft around the center of the map. Updates pause while this page is in the background.

A selected airplane's trail comes from the positions this page has received since it opened, up to the last 3 minutes.

Some aircraft are missing, such as aircraft without ADS-B Out and aircraft flying too low or too far from a receiver to be heard.

Altitude is barometric altitude referenced to 29.92 inches of mercury, so it can differ from an altimeter set to the local altimeter setting. Tracks and bearings are true, not magnetic. Not for navigation.
