How it works

Aircraft equipped with ADS-B Out (Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast) broadcast their GPS position, altitude, ground speed, and callsign once or twice a second. Receivers on the ground, many run by volunteers, pick up those broadcasts and share them with the OpenSky Network. Every 10 seconds, this page asks the OpenSky Network for the aircraft in a box around the center point and draws the ones within the range you picked.

Some aircraft are missing, such as aircraft without ADS-B Out and aircraft flying too low or too far from a receiver to be heard.

Altitude is barometric altitude referenced to 29.92 inches of mercury, so it can differ from an altimeter set to the local altimeter setting. Tracks and bearings are true, not magnetic. Not for navigation.

Without an account, the OpenSky Network allows 400 requests a day. At one request every 10 seconds, that is about 67 minutes of updates. Updates pause while this page is in the background.

Data: The OpenSky Network. Airport locations: OurAirports.
