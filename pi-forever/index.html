π “forever”

1. What is Pi?

Pi, written π, is the number you get when you divide a circle’s circumference by its diameter.

  • Circumference: The distance around the outside of a circle, measured once all the way around. In Figure 1, it is the outline of the circle.
  • Diameter: The length of a straight line that crosses a circle through its center, from one edge to the other. In Figure 1, it is the line across the middle.

In Figure 1, the circle is 10 inches across (the diameter) and about 31.42 inches around (the circumference).

π = circumference ÷ diameter

= 31.42 inches ÷ 10 inches = 3.142

2. The Core Idea

The Magic Ratio: No matter how tiny a coin or how huge a wheel is, its circumference divided by its diameter always gives the same number.

The Short Value: In everyday math, people usually shorten pi to 3.14, or to the fraction 22/7.

The 3-Diameter Rule: If you take the diameter and wrap it around the edge of the circle, it fits 3 times, with a tiny slice of about 0.14 of a diameter left over (Figure 1).
